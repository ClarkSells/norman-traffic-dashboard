Note: `scenes.js`, `camera.js`, `layers/collisions.js`, `ui/popup.js` and `ui/siteview.js` were touched by another agent between 04:01 and 04:05 while this audit ran (git status is clean, so the lead committed); every cited value was re-read after that and line numbers below are from the post 04:05 files, but the screenshots were taken across that window. Renderer note. ## Summary

The bones are right and better than most student work: one type superfamily (Plex Serif, Sans, Mono), a warm paper surface, a validated orange ramp, a sticky ranked table with tabular figures, a real film mode with a storyboard, and a 3D site view that will stop a room. The build already follows a written design direction and a two round QA, and it shows. What stops it reading as a finished operations product is a small number of load bearing defects that a judge or engineer hits in the first thirty seconds: the ranked table's Score column is clipped at 1440 and gone at 390 because the table overflows the 400 px panel by 55 px; the segment popup is 566 px tall and runs 156 px past the bottom of a 900 px viewport so the Forecast block and the "View site in 3D" button, the two things you most want to show, are never visible without panning; the crash density heat layer is on by default and at city zoom it renders as hundreds of blue blobs that bury the orange ranking (the product's own principle 1 says the segments win); opening Simulate or a scenario pushes the ranked table to 118 px and then to zero rows; the site view carries a developer note about "undocumented endpoints" in a city facing screen; and the present mode establish shot frames the whole ward union so Norman's built up grid occupies the left third of a mostly empty stage. Below the P0 and P1 list, the system drift is what makes it feel generated rather than designed: two switch styles (square in explore, round pill in present), two corner radii systems (3 px chrome, 8 and 12 px present, 999 px chips), uppercase mono labels used for everything from eyebrows to legend titles so nothing is quieter than anything else, a `//` code prefix on the eyebrow and the error card, help cards with decorative big numbers, and a legend title that grows to four lines of caps under a scenario. None of these are hard. Most are one line CSS, a camera bounds swap, a default flip, and a copy pass.

## Keep

These already work and must survive any redesign.

1. The type system: Plex Serif 500 for titles, Plex Sans for UI, Plex Mono with tabular numerals for every figure, loaded with preloads and `display: swap` (`index.html:10-14`, `tokens.css:6-8`). It is the main reason the panel reads as an instrument.
2. The paper palette and the validated orange ramp `#f59e5c #ee7b2f #d45f14 #a4480e #6d2f09` with blue `#86b6ef #5598e7 #2a78d6 #1c5cab #0d366b` for crashes (`config.js:34-35`). Orange for the ranking, blue for the evidence is a clean two role scheme.

## Recommended design direction

Format follows the frontend design direction skill: purpose, audience, tone, memorable detail, constraints, then tokens.

**Purpose.** A council briefing instrument that ranks every counted street segment in Norman by congestion and crashes, shows where the ranking comes from, and lets staff test a closure, a game day or a growth year without leaving the map. In present mode it is a ten minute film of the same data.

**Audience.** City of Norman traffic engineers (want the numbers, the method, and a way to look at one corridor fast), councilmembers (want their ward's five), MIS faculty judges (want to see analysis and craft in the same screen). All three will see it first on a projector and later on a laptop; staff will also open it on a phone in the field.

**Tone.** Consulting report, not startup dashboard. Warm paper, ink, one accent hue doing one job. Quiet chrome, loud data. Dense but calm, like a well set engineering drawing title block. Nothing on screen is there because the library offered it.

**Memorable detail.** The square knob. Switches, slider thumbs and the selected row rule are all small squares in ink, so the instrument feels machined, and present mode's progress bar is the same squares laid in a row. Every figure in the product, from the table to the callouts to the site view labels, is set in Plex Mono with tabular numerals, so the numbers look like they came from the same workbook, because they did.

**Constraints.** Mapbox GL JS 3.6 Standard style, no bundler, no new dependencies, ES modules; the orange and blue ramps are validated and fixed; every caption figure comes from `facts.js`; reduced motion must still work as a slide show; WCAG AA for every text token on its surface; one radius scale, one switch, one button border, no gradients except the two present scrims, no icons that are not controls.

Motion rules:

- Only four kinds of motion: camera moves (the four in `camera.js`), the ant path flow, the segment pulse, and the crash reveal. Everything else is a 150 to 250 ms opacity and transform crossfade.

- Durations: 150 fast (hover, switch), 250 mid (card content, legend fold), 350 slow (panel slide, row FLIP), camera 1600 to 2600.
- Curve: `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` out, `cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` in and out for camera.

Copy rules: sentence case everywhere except eyebrows; commas and periods, no dashes; "crashes" in the UI, "collisions" only in method text; a unit on every figure; no developer words on screen (endpoint, pipeline, site.json, stub, sample); scenario state in one chip row, never a paragraph; a caption is one claim and one number.
